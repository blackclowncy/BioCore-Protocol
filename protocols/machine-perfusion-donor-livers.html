Combined Hypothermic and Normothermic Machine Perfusion of Human Donor Livers

biotech Groningen Transplant Center / Nature Protocols • DOI: 10.1038/s41596-024-01130-8

info Overview & Principle

This standardized Nature Protocols method (doi: 10.1038/s41596-024-01130-8) describes the 'back-to-base' combined hypothermic and normothermic machine perfusion procedure for human donor livers using the Liver Assist device (XVIVO). Developed at the Groningen Transplant Center, this protocol enables functional reconditioning, injury mitigation, and rigorous objective viability testing of high-risk extended-criteria donor (ECD) and donation-after-circulatory-death (DCD) organs prior to clinical transplantation.

Three Sequential Perfusion Phases:

  • Phase 1: Dual Hypothermic Oxygenated Perfusion (DHOPE, 8–12°C): Dual low-pressure perfusion (PV 3–5 mmHg, HA 18–25 mmHg) with hyperoxygenated UW-MPS ($pO_2 > 100\text{ kPa}$) replenishes mitochondrial adenosine triphosphate (ATP), downregulates reactive oxygen species (ROS) upon subsequent warm reperfusion, and washes out accumulated succinate.
  • Phase 2: Controlled Oxygenated Rewarming (COR, 20°C → 37°C): Gradual stepwise thermal transition ($3^\circ\text{C}$ every 7.5 min over 45–60 min) with RBC-based perfusate mitigates acute rewarming shock and mitochondrial permeability transition.
  • Phase 3: Normothermic Machine Perfusion (NMP, 37°C): Fully physiological ex situ perfusion ($37^\circ\text{C}$, PV flow $92\text{--}110\text{ mL/min/100g}$, HA flow $\sim 1/3$ PV) restores active hepatocellular and cholangiocellular metabolism, enabling definitive viability assessment within 150 minutes.

format_list_numbered Usage Procedure

Machine Perfusion Circuit Setup & DHOPE Priming

1

Equipment Assembly & Sterility

Mount the Liver Assist disposable set (XVIVO) onto the drive unit. Connect arterial and portal pressure lines, filling lines, and temperature probes in a sterile laminar field.

schedule 10 mins
2

UW Machine Perfusion Solution Priming

Infuse 3 L of sterile UW machine perfusion solution (Belzer MPS) into the circuit through filling lines. Attach a three-way valve to the portal oxygenator for subsequent sampling.

infoDo not detach filling lines after priming; cap them for subsequent refilling during fluid switch.
schedule 15 mins
3

Gas Mixer & Temperature Pre-Cooling

Connect 100% O2 to the gas mixer at 1.0 L/min. Set arterial pressure to 25 mmHg and portal pressure to 5 mmHg. Fill the thermo unit with nonsterile crushed ice to achieve 8–12°C.

schedule 15 mins
4

Blood Gas Oxygenation Verification

Sample perfusate from the sampling port (discarding the first 5 mL dead volume) and analyze with blood gas analyzer. Confirm pO2 > 100 kPa (>750 mmHg) before organ connection.

schedule 5 mins

Donor Liver Preparation & Vessel Cannulation

1

Portal Vein Cannulation

Dissect the portal vein and IVC. Insert the 25 Fr curved cannula into the portal vein oriented toward the right liver lobe without any twisting of the main stem or bifurcation. Secure with braided Mersilene 1.0 thread.

infoDecompress or remove gallbladder if large to prevent portal vein compression.
schedule 15 mins
2

Portal Flush & Air Bubble Elimination

Flush the portal vein with 1 L of cold UW-MPS to wash out static preservation solution. Inspect for leaks. Use a 60 mL catheter-tip syringe to de-air the cannula completely and lock with a hose clamp.

infoAir bubbles will cause airlocks and zero flow during low-pressure hypothermic perfusion.
schedule 10 mins
3

Hepatic Artery Cannulation

Cannulate the 3–4 cm circular supratruncal aorta patch using a 25 Fr straight cannula. Oversew the distal infratruncal aorta with running 2-0 Prolene. (If aortic segment is missing, use a 12 Fr cannula in the celiac trunk or a donor iliac artery interposition graft).

schedule 15 mins
4

Leak Test & Hemostasis

Apply gentle manual pressure with a 60 mL syringe containing UW solution to test the artery for branch leaks. Ligate or clip all bleeding side branches.

schedule 10 mins

Dual Hypothermic Oxygenated Machine Perfusion Phase

1

Liver Placement in Perfusion Chamber

Place an unfolded sterile 40×40 cm gauze on the reservoir net. Position the liver with the dorsal side facing upward and gallbladder oriented toward the machine front.

schedule 5 mins
2

Initiate Portal Perfusion

Connect portal cannula to portal inlet line. Release hose clamp and start portal pump. Adjust pressure to 3–5 mmHg; target flow is 100–300 mL/min (highest flow at lowest pressure).

schedule 5 mins
3

Initiate Arterial Perfusion

Connect arterial cannula to arterial inlet. Start arterial pulsatile perfusion at 18–25 mmHg. Target arterial flow is approximately 1/3 of portal flow.

schedule 5 mins
4

Steady Hypothermic Perfusion (1–2 Hours)

Maintain DHOPE for at least 1 hour (extendable for logistics). Cover vessels with wet cold gauze and close sterile reservoir lid. Perform backtable parenchyma trimming and measure blood gas every 30 mins (pO2 > 100 kPa).

infoDHOPE recharges mitochondrial ATP pools and reduces succinate before rewarming.
schedule 60–120 mins

Perfusate Replacement & Controlled Oxygenated Rewarming

1

Disconnect & Backtable Ringer Flush

Pause pumps, clamp cannulas, and transfer liver to a sterile bowl with cold preservation solution on ice. Flush liver at backtable with 2 L cold Ringer's Lactate (1.5 L PV, 0.5 L HA) to clear high-potassium UW-MPS.

schedule 10 mins
2

Circuit Rinsing & NMP Perfusate Loading

Place flow sensors in water buckets. Vacuum-empty the circuit, rinse with 3 L sterile water, then infuse chosen NMP formulation (Original XVIVO 2,064.7 mL or Modified Lab 1,985 mL) with 10 mL Calcium Gluconate at start.

schedule 15 mins
3

Bile Duct & IVC Cannulation

Cannulate distal common bile duct with an 8 Fr Nutrifit catheter using a 5-0 Prolene purse-string suture. Place a sampling catheter into the IVC cranial orifice.

schedule 10 mins
4

Reconnect at 20°C & Start COR

Reconnect liver at 20°C (PV 5 mmHg, HA 25 mmHg, FiO2 21% at 150–200 mL/min). Ramp temperature upwards by 3°C every 7.5 minutes from 20°C to 37°C over 45–60 minutes (Fig. 2 algorithm).

infoTake arterial blood gas every 7.5 min during rewarming. Maintain mild acidosis during early rewarming to protect against reperfusion injury; correct only if Base Excess < -10 mmol/L.
schedule 45–60 mins

Normothermic Machine Perfusion & Maintenance Protocol

1

Establish Physiological NMP Settings (37°C)

Upon reaching 37°C, adjust portal pressure to 6–11 mmHg and arterial pressure to 35–60 mmHg. Target portal flow is 92–110 mL/min per 100 g liver weight. Start continuous heparin infusion at 1,000 IU/h (0.2 mL/h of 5000 IU/mL).

schedule 10 mins
2

Serial Blood & Bile Sampling (Every 30 min)

Collect arterial blood gas, venous blood gas (target sO2 55–75%), and bile gas every 30 minutes. Collect bile into an Eppendorf tube under mineral oil to prevent atmospheric gas diffusion.

schedule Every 30 mins
3

Perfusate Maintenance & Dynamic Corrections

Monitor arterial glucose (maintain 8–12 mmol/L with 50% glucose boluses), Ca2+ (>=0.70 mmol/L), K+ (3.5–5.0 mmol/L), Cl- (>90 mmol/L), and pH (7.35–7.45) using the Perfusate Correction Guide.

schedule Continuous
4

Transplant Decision & Pre-Implantation Cold Flush

At 150 min NMP, evaluate hepato-biliary viability against Table 3. Continue NMP until recipient surgery is ready. Stop pumps and flush liver with >= 2 L ice-cold preservation solution before implantation.

schedule 15 mins

Original XVIVO Liver Assist Perfusate Formulation

1

Design Principles & Colloid Osmotic Pressure

The XVIVO formulation is designed to provide a physiological colloid osmotic pressure and osmolality tailored for human liver ex situ normothermic machine perfusion. Total initial volume is 2,064.7 mL.

3

Preparation & Loading Sequence

Load RBCs first, followed by Gelofusine, Albumin, sterile water, and pharmaceuticals. Add Sodium Bicarbonate and Calcium Gluconate (10 mL) immediately prior to initiating rewarming/perfusion to avoid calcium-carbonate precipitation.

infoPacked red blood cells are derived from the local blood bank and dissolved in SAGM (saline, adenine, glucose, mannitol) solution.

1

Rationale for Laboratory Substitution

Where clinical products (Gelofusine, TPN N14G30E, Cernevit, Tationil) are restricted or unavailable, this modified formulation provides mass-matched colloid osmotic pressure, stoichiometric electrolyte balance, and robust antimicrobial protection for research and pre-clinical protocols. Total estimated volume: approx 1,985 mL.

Arterial Perfusate & Blood Gas Dynamic Correction Guide during NMP

1

Anticoagulation Maintenance (Heparin 5000 IE/mL)

• Protocol: Administer 0.2 mL (1,000 units) at the start of NMP. Administer 0.2 mL (1,000 units) every hour thereafter (or continuous 1,000 IU/h infusion). • Rationale: Anticoagulation maintains extracorporeal circuit patency and prevents microvascular thrombus formation in the hepatic sinusoids.

schedule Every hour
2

Metabolites: Lactate (cLac) & Glucose (cGluc)

• Lactate (cLac): Target < 1.8 mmol/L after 150 min NMP. (Develops during anaerobic metabolism; a functioning liver clears lactate via gluconeogenesis. Rising lactate indicates severe graft dysfunction). • Glucose (cGluc): Target > 4.0 mmol/L, strive for 8–12 mmol/L. Administer 2–4 mL of Glucose 50% (2.775 mmol/mL) per correction. (Active liver metabolism rapidly clears glucose. Ischemia-reperfusion injury triggers cellular leakage, elevating perfusate glucose).

schedule Every 30 mins
3

Electrolytes: Sodium (cNa⁺), Potassium (cK⁺), Chloride (cCl⁻), Calcium (cCa²⁺)

• Sodium (cNa⁺): Target 135–145 mmol/L. Administer NaCl 5% (0.86 mmol/mL) solely if no NaHCO3 is given, to increase sodium to 135 mmol/L. Initiate correction strictly after T10. (Na+/K+-ATPase pumps Na+ out of cells). • Potassium (cK⁺): Target 3.5–5.0 mmol/L. Administer KCl 74.6 mg/mL (1 mmol/mL) specifically for verified hypokalemia. Apply strict limits to avoid over-correction! (Active Na+/K+-ATPase pumps K+ into cells, depleting perfusate K+). • Chloride (cCl⁻): Target > 90 mmol/L. Administer NaCl 5% (0.86 mmol/mL). (CFTR channel facilitates transmembrane chloride flow toward bile; AE2 exchanges Cl- for HCO3- in bile duct; alkali bile is dependent on Cl- values). • Calcium (cCa²⁺): Target >= 0.70 mmol/L. Administer 10 mL Calcium Gluconate (0.225 mmol/mL) at start of COR, then another 10 mL during normothermic phase when warm. Proceed with 5 mL increments. (Ca2+ is an essential cofactor for bile secretion).

schedule Every 30 mins
4

Blood Gas & Ventilation: pH, pCO2, and pO2

• pH: Target COR < 7.30; NMP: 7.35–7.45. Administer NaHCO3 8.4% (1 mmol/mL) to neutralize acid loads. Note: Mild acidosis during COR protects against reperfusion injury. • pCO2: Target 4.5–6.0 kPa. Decrease gas flow to lower pH (increase pCO2); increase gas flow to raise pH (accelerate pCO2 clearance). Caution: Only increase gas flow once liver produces CO2. • pO2: Target continuous levels > 10 kPa (10–13.3 kPa during COR & NMP). Elevate FiO2 in 1–2% increments concomitantly with gas flow. (Hypoxia is toxic; hyperoxia generates harmful reactive oxygen species).

infoArterial blood gas samples should be taken every 7.5 min during rewarming and every 30 min during NMP.
schedule Every 30 mins

Groningen Hepato-Biliary Viability Criteria (Table 3)

1

Hepatocellular Criteria (Metabolic Recovery)

1. Bile Production: >= 10 g total within 150 min (with >= 4 g in the final hour). 2. Perfusate Lactate Clearance: < 1.7 mmol/L at 150 min. 3. Perfusate pH: 7.35–7.45.

infoRapid lactate clearance reflects robust mitochondrial beta-oxidation and gluconeogenesis.
schedule 150 mins
2

Cholangiocellular Criteria (Bile Duct Integrity)

1. Bile pH: > 7.45 (and Delta pH > 0.10 above perfusate). 2. Delta Bicarbonate (Bile - Perfusate): > 5.0 mmol/L. 3. Delta Glucose (Bile - Perfusate): < -5.0 mmol/L (reflecting active cholangiocyte glucose reabsorption).

infoAlkaline biliary pH and bicarbonate excretion protect cholangiocytes against cytotoxic hydrophobic bile salts.
schedule 150 mins
3

Clinical Decision Framework

GREEN ZONE: Proceed to transplantation with low risk of ischemic cholangiopathy or primary non-function. ORANGE ZONE: Borderline function; transplant only if other criteria are solid green and recipient urgency warrants. RED ZONE: Reject organ (high risk of graft failure).

schedule Decision

Troubleshooting Complications & Technical Errors (Table 4)

1

Low Flow / High Vascular Resistance

Check for vessel kinking, airlocks in cannula (aspirate with 60 mL syringe), or IVC outflow obstruction by gauze. Reposition cannula with braided thread. If liver is higher than pressure sensor, lower net in reservoir.

schedule Immediate
2

Excessive Non-Physiological High Flow

Most common cause is side branch leak or intimal dissection. Inspect all arterial and portal branches; apply clips or sutures to stop leakage.

schedule Immediate
3

Hypoxia / Dark Perfusate

Check venous sO2 (<55%) and arterial pO2 (<10 kPa). Increase FiO2 by 1–2% increments or check oxygenator gas line connections.

schedule Immediate
4

Persistent Lactic Acidosis during NMP

Indicates parenchymal hypoperfusion, cellular necrosis, or oxygenation failure. Verify vascular flows, check temperature calibration, and evaluate viability thresholds.

schedule Assessment

warning Cautions & Safety

  • check_circle Maintain strict sterility throughout all phases; take perfusate samples for microbiological culture at start and end of NMP.
  • check_circle Never allow air bubbles into the portal or arterial cannulas during connection to avoid catastrophic airlocks.
  • check_circle Do not correct pH aggressively during early rewarming; a mildly acidic environment protects against reperfusion injury.
  • check_circle When using near-expired RBCs, omit baseline KCl to prevent dangerous hyperkalemia (>6.0 mmol/L).
  • check_circle Always flush the donor liver with >= 2 L cold preservation solution upon stopping NMP to clear the blood perfusate prior to implantation.
analytics

Expected Results

Restores cellular ATP levels, clears perfusate lactate to < 1.7 mmol/L within 150 minutes, produces >= 10 g alkaline bile with active bicarbonate secretion, and allows safe transplantation of previously discarded high-risk DCD/ECD livers.
