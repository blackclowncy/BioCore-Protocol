Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT/GPT) Activity Assay Kit

biotech Invitrogen / Thermo Fisher Scientific • Catalog Number EEA001 (96 tests)

info Overview & Principle

Alanine aminotransferase (ALT/GPT) is widely distributed in plasma and various body tissues, including liver, kidney, heart, and skeletal muscle. Clinically, serum ALT activity serves as an essential biomarker for hepatocellular injury, viral hepatitis, and cirrhosis.

Assay Principle: ALT catalyzes the transamination between L-alanine and α-ketoglutaric acid to generate pyruvic acid and L-glutamic acid at pH 7.4 and 37°C. Phenylhydrazine is then added to react with pyruvic acid, forming a reddish-brown phenylhydrazone complex under alkaline conditions. ALT activity is quantified by measuring absorbance at 510 nm.

format_list_numbered Usage Procedure

Standard Curve Preparation

1

Dilute Sodium Pyruvate Standard

Dilute the 2 mmol/L Pyruvate standard with distilled water to generate standard concentrations corresponding to Carmen units of 0, 28, 57, 97, 150, and 200.

infoPrepare fresh standard dilutions before every assay run.
schedule 10 mins
2

Add Standard & Substrate to Wells

Pipette 20 µL of each standard dilution and 50 µL of Substrate Solution into respective wells of the 96-well microplate.

schedule 5 mins
3

Add Developer Reagent

Add 50 µL of Developer Reagent to each well. Mix thoroughly by gentle tapping or plate shaker.

schedule 2 mins
4

Incubation

Incubate the microplate at 37°C for 20 minutes protected from light.

schedule 20 mins
5

Add Stop/Alkali Reagent & Read OD

Add 150 µL of Alkali Solution to each well. Incubate at room temperature for 10 minutes, then read absorbance at 510 nm immediately.

infoMeasure within 15 minutes after color development.
schedule 10 mins

Sample Measurement Procedure (Serum / Tissue)

1

Sample Preparation

Serum/plasma: Use directly or dilute with PBS if ALT activity exceeds 100 U/L. Tissue: Homogenize in ice-cold PBS (1:9 w/v), centrifuge at 10,000 × g (4°C) for 10 mins, collect supernatant.

infoKeep all tissue homogenates on ice.
schedule 15 mins
2

Add Reagents to Sample & Control Wells

Sample Well: 20 µL sample + 50 µL Substrate Solution. Control Well (Blank): 20 µL sample (add substrate after incubation or add water blank).

schedule 5 mins
3

Enzymatic Reaction Incubation

Incubate the microplate in a 37°C incubator or water bath for precisely 30 minutes.

schedule 30 mins
4

Color Development & Termination

Add 50 µL Developer Reagent, incubate 20 mins at 37°C. Add 150 µL Alkali Solution, incubate 10 mins at RT, and measure OD at 510 nm.

schedule 30 mins

calculate Standard Curve & Mathematical Calculation (EEA001)

Serum: ALT (IU/L) = [a·(ΔA510)² + b·(ΔA510) + c] × 0.482 × f Tissue: ALT (IU/gprot) = [a·(ΔA510)² + b·(ΔA510) + c] × 0.482 × f / Cpr

1. Standard Curve Formulation: Plot Carmen units (0, 28, 57, 97, 150, 200) as y-axis against absolute standard OD ($x = OD_{std} - OD_{blank}$) as x-axis. Fit quadratic curve: $y = ax^2 + bx + c$. Example calibrated coefficients: a = 2593.62, b = 21.38, c = 1.06.

2. Net Sample Absorbance ($\Delta A_{510}$): $\Delta A_{510} = OD_{sample} - OD_{control}$.

3. Unit Definition & Conversion: 1 Carmen unit is defined as the amount of enzyme producing 1 µmol pyruvate under assay conditions. 1 Carmen unit = 0.482 IU/L (or µmol/min/L). $f$ = sample dilution factor; $C_{pr}$ = tissue protein concentration (gprot/L).

Storage & Stability

Kit and components shipped and stored at -20°C. Once opened, reagents stable for 1 month at 2-8°C.

event_available Stable for 12 months at -20°C.

warning Cautions & Safety

  • check_circle Protect developer and substrate solutions from direct intense light.
  • check_circle If the OD value exceeds the standard curve range, dilute sample with PBS and re-test.
  • check_circle Alkali reagent is caustic; wear safety glasses and nitrile gloves.
analytics

Expected Results

Normal serum ALT in rodents and humans is typically < 40–50 U/L. Elevated levels (> 100–1000 U/L) indicate acute liver injury or drug-induced hepatotoxicity.
